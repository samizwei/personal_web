Bio

Theoretical physicist interested in strongly correlated system, particularly superconductivity and magnetism, computational methods and machine learning.

  • About me
  • Beyond Research
I am a PhD student in the Condensed Matter Theory section at the Niels Bohr Institute working with Morten Holm Christensen. My research focuses on renormalization group approaches combined with group-theoretical modeling and quantum Monte Carlo simulations to elucidate the role of anisotropic electronic interactions in the interplay between magnetic and superconducting instabilities.
Prior to this, I obtained a Master’s degree in Physics, with research interests in condensed matter theory, computational physics, and machine learning. My work has centered on the study of correlated quantum systems using variational and numerical techniques, including Variational Monte Carlo (VMC), Neural Quantum States (NQS), and the Density Matrix Renormalization Group (DMRG), alongside analytical methods.
I completed my Master’s thesis under the supervision of Juan Carrasquilla, where I investigated a triangular quantum antiferromagnet using neural quantum states and variational Monte Carlo methods.
More broadly, I am fascinated by the intersection of mathematics, physics, and numerical methods, and by how modern computational and machine learning techniques can be applied not only to understand quantum many-body systems and materials, but also to address problems in fields such as finance and insurance.
Beyond academic research, I have industry experience as an R&D intern at Sensirion AG, where I worked on thermal conductivity (TC) sensors, and as an actuarial intern at Zurich Insurance, contributing to data-driven analysis and modeling tasks.
When I am not working I enjoy being active and spending time in the mountains 🏔️ hiking, bouldering 🧗🏻‍♂️, and snowboarding 🏂. But I also enjoy staying at home cooking, reading or playing board games with friends.
